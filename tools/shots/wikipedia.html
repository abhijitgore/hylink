Ferris wheel

From Openpedia, the free encyclopedia

Ferris wheel

Also known asBig wheel, observation wheel
Named afterGeorge W. G. Ferris Jr.
Debut1893, Chicago
TypeAmusement ride

A Ferris wheel is an amusement ride consisting of a rotating upright wheel with multiple passenger-carrying cabins, called gondolas or pods, attached to the rim in such a way that as the wheel turns, they are kept upright, usually by gravity, and sometimes by a motor. Some Ferris wheels are described as observation wheels, built primarily to give riders a view of the surrounding area.

The original Ferris wheel was designed and built by George Washington Gale Ferris Jr. as a landmark for the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago, which celebrated the 400th anniversary of Christopher Columbus's voyage. The exposition committee wanted something to rival the Eiffel Tower, built for the 1889 Paris exposition, and Ferris's wheel — 80.4 metres tall, with 36 wooden cars each able to hold 60 people — met the brief.

Later wheels grew taller and switched from wood to steel, and many modern observation wheels use permanently enclosed, climate-controlled cabins rather than open cars. The tallest examples today exceed 150 metres, roughly double the height of the original.

Design

A Ferris wheel's rim is typically supported by a network of spokes running to a central hub, similar in principle to a bicycle wheel, though very large modern wheels sometimes use a cable-supported rim instead to save weight. Gondolas hang from the rim on bearings that let them rotate freely, keeping the floor level as the wheel turns — the same effect a gimbal has on a ship's compass.

Rotation speed is deliberately slow: a full revolution on most large wheels takes somewhere between fifteen and thirty minutes, which keeps loading and unloading simple and gives riders a long, steady view rather than a brief one.
